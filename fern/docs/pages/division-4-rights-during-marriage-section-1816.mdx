# 1816
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  For purposes of this section, the following definitions apply:

<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  “Eligible provider” means the Administrative Office of the Courts or an educational institution, professional association, professional continuing education group, a group connected to the courts, or a public or private group that has been authorized by the Administrative Office of the Courts to provide domestic violence training.

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  “Evaluator” means a supervising or associate counselor described in Section 1815, a mediator described in Section 3164, a court-connected or private child custody evaluator described in Section 3110.5, or a court-appointed investigator or evaluator as described in Section 3110 or Section 730 of the Evidence Code.

<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  An evaluator shall participate in a program of continuing instruction in domestic violence, including child abuse, as may be arranged and provided to that evaluator. This training may utilize domestic violence training programs conducted by nonprofit community organizations with an expertise in domestic violence issues.

<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Areas of basic instruction shall include, but are not limited to, the following:

<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The effects on children.

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The nature and extent .

<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The social and family dynamics .

<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Techniques for identifying and assisting families affected by domestic violence.

<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  Interviewing, documentation of, and appropriate recommendations for, families affected by domestic violence.

<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  The legal rights of, and remedies available to, victims.

<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  Availability of community and legal domestic violence resources.

<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  An evaluator shall also complete 16 hours of advanced training within a 12-month period. Four hours of that advanced training shall include community resource networking intended to acquaint the evaluator with domestic violence resources in the geographical communities where the family being evaluated may reside. Twelve hours of instruction, as approved by the Administrative Office of the Courts, shall include all of the following:

<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The appropriate structuring of the child custody evaluation process, including, but not limited to, all of the following:

<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Maximizing safety for clients, evaluators, and court personnel.

<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Maintaining objectivity.

<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  Providing and gathering balanced information from the parties and controlling for bias.

<span class="subsec">(D)</span>  Providing separate sessions at separate times as described in Section 3113.

<span class="subsec">(E)</span>  Considering the impact of the evaluation report and recommendations with particular attention to the dynamics .

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The relevant sections of local, state, and federal laws, rules, or regulations.

<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The range, availability, and applicability resources available to victims, including, but not limited to, all of the following:

<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Domestic violence shelter-based programs.

<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Counseling, including drug and alcohol counseling.

<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  Legal assistance.

<span class="subsec">(D)</span>  Job training.

<span class="subsec">(E)</span>  Parenting classes.

<span class="subsec">(F)</span>  Resources for a victim who is an immigrant.

<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  The range, availability, and applicability intervention available to perpetrators, including, but not limited to, all of the following:

<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Certified treatment programs described in subdivision <span class="subsec">(c)</span>  of Section 1203.097 of the Penal Code.

<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Drug and alcohol counseling.

<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  Legal assistance.

<span class="subsec">(D)</span>  Job training.

<span class="subsec">(E)</span>  Parenting classes.

<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The unique issues in a family and psychological assessment in a domestic violence case, including all of the following:

<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  The effects of exposure to domestic violence and psychological trauma on children, the relationship between child physical abuse, child sexual abuse, and domestic violence, the differential family dynamics related to parent-child attachments in families with domestic violence, intergenerational transmission of familial violence, and manifestations of post-traumatic stress disorders in children.

<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  The nature and extent , and the relationship of gender, class, race, culture, and sexual orientation to domestic violence.

<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  Current legal, psychosocial, public policy, and mental health research related to the dynamics of family violence, the impact of victimization, the psychology of perpetration, and the dynamics of power and control in battering relationships.

<span class="subsec">(D)</span>  The assessment of family history based on the type, severity, and frequency of violence.

<span class="subsec">(E)</span>  The impact on parenting abilities of being a victim or perpetrator .

<span class="subsec">(F)</span>  The uses and limitations of psychological testing and psychiatric diagnosis in assessing parenting abilities in domestic violence cases.

<span class="subsec">(G)</span>  The influence of alcohol and drug use and abuse on the incidence .

<span class="subsec">(H)</span>  Understanding the dynamics of high conflict relationships and relationships between an abuser and victim.

<span class="subsec">(I)</span>  The importance of, and procedures for, obtaining collateral information from a probation department, children’s protective services, police incident report, a pleading regarding a restraining order, medical records, a school, and other relevant sources.

<span class="subsec">(J)</span>  Accepted methods for structuring safe and enforceable child custody and parenting plans that ensure the health, safety, welfare, and best interest of the child, and safeguards for the parties.

<span class="subsec">(K)</span>  The importance of discouraging participants in child custody matters from blaming victims for the violence and from minimizing allegations , child abuse, or abuse against a family member.

<span class="subsec">(L)</span>  The risks associated with access to firearms and ways to reduce those risks.
