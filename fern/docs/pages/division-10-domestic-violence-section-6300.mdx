<strong>J.J. v. M.F. (2014)</strong>

*223 Cal.App.4th*

This case defined “primary aggressor” for the first time in the context of mutual restraining orders: a trial court must look at the larger context of the parties’ relationship. Here, M.F. and J.J. filed restraining orders against one another. M.F. had pushed J.J. out of a car, pushed her through a glass door, and sent her threatening text messages. Also, when M.F. was returning their child’s jacket, he strangled J.J., dragged her, and caused her bodily injury. The court entered mutual restraining orders, finding that J.J. had also been a primary aggressor by repeatedly phoning M.F. about the jacket in a harassing fashion, and pushing him away when he approached and tried to snatch their son.
