# § 5272 — Arrearage; modification of order

arrearage; modification of order
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  If an assignment order is sought under Article 3
(commencing with Section 5250), the party ordered to pay
support may also move to quash the service of the order
based upon Section 5260.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  The obligor shall state under oath the ground on which
the motion to quash is made.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  If an assignment order which has been issued and
served on a prior employer is served on the obligor’s new
employer, the obligor does not have the right to move to
quash the assignment order on any grounds which the obligor
previously raised when the assignment order was served on
the prior employer or on any grounds which the obligor could
have raised when the assignment order was served on the
prior employer but failed to raise.
(Stats.1992, c. 162
(A.B.2650), § 10, operative Jan. 1, 1994.)
A finding of error in the amount of the current support or
arrearage or that the amount exceeds federal or state limits is
not grounds to vacate the assignment order. The court shall
modify the order to reflect the correct or allowable amount of
support or arrearages. The fact that the obligor may have
subsequently paid the arrearages does not relieve the court of
its duty to enter the assignment order. (Stats.1992, c. 162
(A.B.2650), § 10, operative Jan. 1, 1994.)
