# 811 — Deficits in mental functions

Premarital Agreements

Deficits in mental functions
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  A determination that a person is of unsound mind or
lacks the capacity to make a decision or do a certain act,
including, but not limited to, the incapacity to contract, to
make a conveyance, to marry, to make medical decisions, to
execute wills, or to execute trusts, shall be supported by
evidence of a deficit in at least one of the following mental
functions, subject to subdivision (b), and evidence of a
correlation between the deficit or deficits and the decision or
acts in question:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Alertness and attention, including, but not limited to,
the following:
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Level of arousal or consciousness.
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Orientation to time, place, person, and situation.
<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  Ability to attend and concentrate.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Information processing, including, but not limited to,
the following:
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Short- and long-term memory, including immediate
recall.
understanding, or is of unsound mind, or suffers from one or
more mental deficits so substantial that, under the circum-
stances, the person should be deemed to lack the legal
capacity to perform a specific act, should be based on
evidence of a deficit in one or more of the person’s mental
functions rather than on a diagnosis of a person’s mental or
physical disorder.
(Added by Stats.1995, c. 842 (S.B.730),
