### Part 1. Definitions and General Provisions

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 3500–3830</Badge>

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="§ 3500. Definitions govern">
    Unless the provision or context otherwise requires, the definitions in this chapter govern the construction of this division.

    *(Enacted by Stats. 1992, Ch. 162, Sec. 10. Operative January 1, 1994.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 3 — Support Agreements (§§ 3580–3593)">
    Parents and spouses may enter support agreements. Child support agreements are subject to court approval in the child's best interest; spousal support agreements are enforceable as contracts.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 6 — Modification / Termination (§§ 3650–3693)">
    Support orders may be modified on a showing of changed circumstances. A simplified procedure (§ 3680 et seq.) applies in many cases.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Anchor id="part-2">

### Part 2. Child Support

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 3900–4253</Badge>

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="§ 3900. Duty of parents">
    Subject to this division, the father and mother of a minor child have an equal responsibility to support their child in the manner suitable to the child's circumstances.

    *(Enacted by Stats. 1992, Ch. 162, Sec. 10. Operative January 1, 1994.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 4050. Purpose of the statewide uniform guideline">
    In adopting the statewide uniform guideline provided in this article, it is the intention of the Legislature to ensure that this state remains in compliance with federal regulations for child support guidelines.

    *(Repealed and added by Stats. 1993, Ch. 219, Sec. 138. Effective January 1, 1994.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 4053. Principles of the guideline">
    In implementing the statewide uniform guideline, the courts shall adhere to the following principles: (a) A parent's first and principal obligation is to support the parent's minor children according to the parent's circumstances and station in life. (b) Both parents are mutually responsible for the support of their children. (c) The guideline takes into account each parent's actual income and level of responsibility for the children. ... (e) The guideline seeks to place the interests of children as the state's top priority. (f) Children should share in the standard of living of both parents... (k) The guideline is intended to be presumptively correct in all cases, and only under special circumstances should child support orders fall below the child support mandated by the guideline formula.

    *(Amended by Stats. 2019, Ch. 115, Sec. 44. (AB 1817) Effective January 1, 2020.)*
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

#### The guideline formula — § 4055

<Note>
  The statewide uniform guideline is <Copy>CS = K[HN - (H%)(TN)]</Copy>. The components:
  <strong>CS</strong> = child support; <strong>K</strong> = share of income allocated to support;
  <strong>HN</strong> = high earner's net monthly disposable income; <strong>H%</strong> = high earner's
  approximate percentage of time with the children; <strong>TN</strong> = total net monthly disposable income.
</Note>

The multiplier **K** is set by total net disposable income:

| Total Net Disposable Income / Month | K |
| --- | --- |
| $0–2,900 | 0.165 + TN/82,857 |
| $2,901–5,000 | 0.131 + TN/42,149 |
| $5,001–10,000 | 0.250 |
| $10,001–15,000 | 0.10 + 1,499/TN |
| Over $15,000 | 0.12 + 1,200/TN |

For more than one child, multiply CS by: 2 children ×1.6, 3 ×2.0, 4 ×2.3, 5 ×2.5, 6 ×2.625, 7 ×2.75, 8 ×2.813, 9 ×2.844, 10 ×2.86.

```text title="Family Code § 4055 (a) — guideline formula"
CS = K [ HN - (H%)(TN) ]

CS  = child support amount
K   = share of income allocated for support (see table)
HN  = high earner's net monthly disposable income
H%  = high earner's approximate % of time with children
TN  = total net monthly disposable income of both parties
```

<Warning title="Presumptively correct">
  Under § 4053(k), the guideline formula is <strong>presumptively correct in all cases</strong>.
  An order below the formula amount is allowed only under special circumstances (§ 4055(b)(7) low-income adjustment, etc.).
</Warning>

### Part 3. Spousal Support

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 4300–4360</Badge>

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="§ 4300. Duty to support spouse">
    Subject to this division, a person shall support the person's spouse.

    *(Enacted by Stats. 1992, Ch. 162, Sec. 10. Operative January 1, 1994.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 4320. Factors in ordering spousal support">
    In ordering spousal support under this part, the court shall consider all of the following circumstances: (a) The extent to which the earning capacity of each party is sufficient to maintain the standard of living established during the marriage... (b) The extent to which the supported party contributed to the attainment of an education, training, a career position, or a license by the supporting party. (c) The ability of the supporting party to pay... (f) The duration of the marriage. (l) The goal that the supported party shall be self-supporting within a reasonable period of time. Except in the case of a marriage of long duration... a "reasonable period of time"... generally shall be one-half the length of the marriage.

    *(Amended by Stats. 2018, Ch. 938, Sec. 1. (AB 929) Effective January 1, 2019.)*
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Anchor id="part-4">

### Part 4. Support of Parents

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 4400–4414</Badge>

</Anchor>

Adult children may have a duty to support a parent who is in need and unable to maintain themselves (§§ 4400–4405), with relief where a parent abandoned the child (§§ 4410–4414).

---

<Anchor id="part-5">

### Part 5. Enforcement of Support Orders

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 4500–5616</Badge>

</Anchor>

The largest Part of the Division: earnings assignment orders (Chapter 8, §§ 5200–5604), child support delinquency reporting (Chapter 4), civil penalties (Chapter 5), and private child support collectors (Chapter 9).

---

<Anchor id="part-6">

### Part 6. Uniform Interstate Family Support Act

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 5700.101–5700.905</Badge>

</Anchor>

California's adoption of UIFSA governs jurisdiction, establishment, enforcement, and modification of support orders across state and international lines.
