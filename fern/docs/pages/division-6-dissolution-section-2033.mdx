# 2033. Family law attorney’s real property lien; notice;

*Nullity, Dissolution, and Legal Separation*

Family law attorney’s real property lien; notice;
objections
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  Either party may encumber the party’s interest
in community real property to pay reasonable attorney’s fees
in order to retain or maintain legal counsel in a proceeding
for dissolution of marriage, for nullity of marriage, or for
legal separation of the parties.
This encumbrance shall be
known as a “family law attorney’s real property lien” and
attaches only to the encumbering party’s interest in the
community real property.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  Notice of a family law attorney’s real property lien
shall be served either personally or on the other party’s
attorney of record at least 15 days before the encumbrance is
recorded.
This notice shall contain a declaration signed
under penalty of perjury containing all of the following:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  A full description of the real property.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The party’s belief as to the fair market value of the
property and documentation supporting that belief.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Encumbrances on the property as of the date of the
declaration.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  A list of community assets and liabilities and their
estimated values as of the date of the declaration.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The amount of the family law attorney’s real property
lien.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  The nonencumbering party may file an ex parte
objection to the family law attorney’s real property lien. The
objection shall include a request to stay the recordation until
further notice of the court and shall contain a copy of the
notice received.
The objection shall also include a declara-
tion signed under penalty of perjury as to all of the following:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Specific objections to the family law attorney’s real
property
lien and to the specific items
in. the notice.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The objector’s belief as to the appropriate items or
value and documentation supporting that belief.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  A declaration specifically stating why recordation of
the encumbrance
at this time would likely result in an

GENERAL PROVISIONS
unequal division of property or would otherwise be unjust
under the circumstances of the case.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  Except as otherwise provided by this section, general
procedural rules regarding ex parte motions apply.
<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  An attorney for whom a family law attorney’s real
property lien is obtained shall comply with Rule 3-300 of the
Rules of Professional Conduct of the State Bar of California.
