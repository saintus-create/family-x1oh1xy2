[Revised Comment]
The California Supreme Court denied review and depublished
Fischer v. Fisher, 232 Cal.Rptr.3d 621 (2018), which purported to hold
that a trial court did not abuse its discretion by denying a husband’s
request for a section 6320 domestic violence order when his wife
slapped and pushed him immediately after discovering that he had
lied to her about his persistent sexual infidelity and there was no
likelihood that her behavior would recur. The court of appeal
reasoned that, under this section, abuse requires some degree of
intent to cause or attempt to cause bodily injury and the wife’s
immediate reaction to her discovery of her husband’s betrayal was
not the result of conscious and deliberate intention. Fischer would
have extended the requirement of deliberate intention to subsection
(a)(4) behavior that could be enjoined under section 6320, even
though the latter section does not require any degree of intent.
Applying subsection (c), Guo v. Xiao, 228 Cal.App.4th 812, 175
Cal. Rptr.3d 635 (2014), held that a wife could be a victim of violence
based on her husband’s physical abuse of the parties’ son and, under
section 6320(a), the consequential disturbance of her peace.
People v. Kovacich, 201 Cal.App.4th 863, 133 Cal.Rptr.3d 924
(2011), review denied, held that a criminal defendant’s violent assault
on a family dog in front of his wife and children constituted abuse
under this section.

Construction of code; application of definitions
the provision
“Abuse” defined
(a) For purposes of this act, “abuse” means any of the
following:
(1) To intentionally or recklessly cause or attempt to cause
bodily injury.
(2) Sexual assault.
(3) To place a person in reasonable apprehension of
imminent serious bodily injury to that person or to another.
(4) To engage in any behavior that has been or could be
enjoined pursuant to Section 6320.
