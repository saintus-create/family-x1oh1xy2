J. v. Christopher U. (2023) 96 Cal.App.5th 108 In this case, Parris got a five-year DVRO against her former husband Christopher, and an award of $200,000 attorney’s fees under the DVPA. The trial court also denied Christopher’s two requests for a Statement of Decision. The Court of Appeal affirmed, concluding the trial court correctly found the evidence showed Christopher had abused Parris in many ways, including financial abuse, threats, coercive control, harassment, and disturbing the peace. Plus, Christopher violated Parris’s TRO by sending disparaging letters to her new employer, trying to get her fired. Ex parte order enjoining specified behavior
The court may issue an ex parte order enjoining a party
from specified behavior that the court determines is necessary
to effectuate orders under Section 6320 or 6321.
