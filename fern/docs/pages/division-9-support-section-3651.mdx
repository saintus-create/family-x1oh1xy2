In ‘re Marriage of T.C. & D.C., 30 Cal.App.5th 419, 241 Cal.Rptr.3d
450, affirmed a trial court’s finding that a “dramatic” increase in a
former wife’s salary was a material change of circumstances that
justified a downward modification of her support obligation, which
required her to pay her husband a percentage of her annual salary
above $180,00. However, the appellate court held that the trial court
erred in calculating the modification because it did not take into
account the parties’ reasonable expectations, which were expressed in
their marital settlement agreement. Close reading of the facts
suggests that the appellate court also may have erred in determining
that the earnings increase was “dramatic” or beyond the parties’
reasonable expectations in light of her entire marital earnings history.
In re Marriage of Khera and Sameer, 206 Cal.App.4th 1464, 143
Cal.Rptr.3d 81 (2012), held that a wife’s post-divorce decision to
pursue a doctoral degree instead of completing her MSW degree and
entering the job market, as contemplated by the parties’ marital
settlement agreement, was not a change of circumstances justifying
the modification of a Richmond step-down spousal support order.
For further discussion of step-down orders, see Commentary to
section 3591.
In re Marriage of Mosley, 165 Cal.App.4th 1375, 82 Cal.Rptr.3d 479
(2008), held that a trial court erred in denying an ex-spouse’s request
to modify his child and spousal support obligation based on changed
ment.
