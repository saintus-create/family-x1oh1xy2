<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  When the parties do not consent, the court may, upon
the written motion of any party, or of its own motion, appoint
a referee in the following cases pursuant to the provisions of
subdivision <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  of Section 640:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  When the trial of an issue of fact requires the
examination of a long account on either side; in which case
the referees may be directed to hear and decide the whole
issue, or report upon any specific question of fact involved
therein.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  When the taking of an account is necessary for the
information of the court before judgment, or for carrying a
judgment or order into effect.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  When a question of fact, other than upon the pleadings, arises upon motion or otherwise, in any stage of the
action.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  When it is necessary for the information of the court in
a special proceeding.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  When the court in any pending action determines that
it is necessary for the court to appoint a referee to hear and
determine any and all discovery motions and disputes relevant to discovery in the action and to report findings and
make a recommendation thereon.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  In a discovery matter, a motion to disqualify an
appointed referee pursuant to Section 170.6 shall be made to
the court by a party either:
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Within 10 days after notice of the appointment, or if
the party has not yet appeared in the action, a motion shall be
made within 10 days after the appearance, if a discovery
referee has been appointed for all. discovery purposes.
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  At least five days before the date set for hearing, if the
referee assigned is known at least 10 days before the date set
for hearing and the discovery referee has been assigned only
for limited discovery purposes.
;
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  When a referee is appointed pursuant to paragraph <span class="subsec">(5)</span> 
of subdivision (a), the order shall indicate whether the
referee is being appointed for all discovery purposes in the
action.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  All appointments of referees pursuant to this section
shall be by written order and shall include the following:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  When the referee is appointed pursuant to paragraph
(1), (2), (3), or <span class="subsec">(4)</span>  of subdivision (a), a statement of the
reason the referee is being appointed.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  When the referee is appointed pursuant to paragraph
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  of subdivision (a), the exceptional circumstances requiring
the reference, which must be specific to the circumstances of
the particular case.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The subject matter or matters included in the reference.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  The name, business address, and telephone number of
the referee.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The maximum hourly rate the referee may charge and,
at the request of any party, the maximum number of hours

(6)<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Either a finding that no party has established an

findings is made.
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  In determining whether a party has established an
inability to pay the referee’s fees under subparagraph (A), the
court shall consider only the ability of the party, not the
party’s counsel, to pay these fees. Ifa party is proceeding in
forma pauperis, the party shall be deemed by the court to
have an economic inability to pay the referee’s fees. However, a determination of economic inability to pay the fees shall
not be limited to parties that proceed in forma pauperis. For
those parties who are not proceeding in forma pauperis, the
court, in determining whether a party has established an
inability to pay the fees, shall consider, among other things,
the estimated cost of the referral and the impact of the
proposed fees on the party’s ability to proceed with the
litigation.
