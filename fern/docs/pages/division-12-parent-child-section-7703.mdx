<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Qualifying child.—For purposes of this section— 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  In general.—The term “qualifying child” means a qualifying child of the taxpayer (as defined in section 152(c)) who has not attained age 17. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Exception for certain noncitizens——The term 
“qualifying child” shall not include any individual who would not be a dependent if subparagraph (A) of section 152(b)(3) were applied without regard to all that follows “resident of the United States”.

<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  the amount by which the aggregate amount of credits allowed by this subpart (determined without regard to this subsection) would increase if the limitation imposed by section 26(a) were increased by the greater of—

<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  Identification requirements.— | 

 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Qualifying child identification requirement.—No credit shall be allowed under this section to a taxpayer with respect to any qualifying child unless the taxpayer includes the name and taxpayer identification number of such qualifying child on the return of tax for the taxable year and such taxpayer identification number was issued on or before the due date for filing such return. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Taxpayer identification requirement.—No credit shall be allowed under this section if the taxpayer identification number of the taxpayer was issued after the due date for filing the return for the taxable year. 
<span class="subsec">(f)</span>  Taxable year must be full taxable year.—Except in the case of a taxable year closed by reason of the death of the taxpayer, no credit shall be allowable under this section in the case of a taxable year covering a period of less than 12 months. 
<span class="subsec">(g)</span>  Restrictions on taxpayers who improperly claimed credit in prior year.— 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Taxpayers making prior fraudulent or reckless claims.— 
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  In general.—No credit shall be allowed under this section for any taxable year in the disallowance period. 
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Disallowance period.—For purposes of subparagraph (A), the disallowance period is— 
<span class="subsec">(i)</span>  the period of 10 taxable years after the most recent taxable year for which there was a final determination that the taxpayer’s claim of credit under this section was due to fraud, and 
<span class="subsec">(ii)</span>  the period of 2 taxable years after the most recent taxable year for which there was a final determination that the taxpayer’s claim of credit under this section was due to reckless or intentional disregard of rules and regulations 
(but not due to fraud). 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Taxpayers making improper prior claims.—In the case of a taxpayer who is denied credit under this section for any taxable year as a result of the deficiency procedures under subchapter B of chapter 63, no credit shall be allowed under this section for any subsequent taxable year unless the taxpayer provides such information as the Secretary may require to demonstrate eligibility for such credit. 
<span class="subsec">(h)</span>  Special rules for taxable years 2018 through 2025.— 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  In general.—In the case of a taxable year beginning after December 31, 2017, and before January 1, 2026, this section shall be applied as provided in paragraphs (2) through (7). 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Credit amount.—Subsection (a) shall be applied by substituting “$2,000” for “$1,000”. 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Limitation.—In lieu of the amount determined under subsection (b)(2), the threshold amount shall be 
‘$400,000 in the case of a joint return ($200,000 in any other case).
