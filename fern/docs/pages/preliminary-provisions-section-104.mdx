# 104 — In this situation, the spouses’

Preliminary Provisions and Definitions

In this situation, the spouses’
traditional community property right of testamentary disposition is
substantially preserved by the unilateral power of revocation.
See
subdivision (b). Where the trust requires joint action for revocation,
the trust could preserve the power of testamentary disposition by
granting the first spouse to die a testamentary power of modification,
appointment, or disposition as to the spouse’s share of the communi-
ty property.
Subdivision <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  establishes the presumption that either spouse
acting alone may revoke the trust as to the community property.
The statute makes clear, however, that a unilateral revocation does
not change the community property character of property received by
the revoking spouse.
Subdivision <span class="subsec">(c)</span>  makes clear that the trustee may manage the trust
community property in the same manner as other trust assets, free
from the general limitations on disposition of community property
imposed on spouses, unless the trust expressly provides such limita-
tions.
Section 761 is not restrictive and does not provide the exclusive
means by which community property may be held in trust without loss
of its community character.
See subdivision (e).
[23 Cal.L.Rev.
Comm. Reports 1 (1993)].
Commentary
For general discussion of Section 761, see Estate of Wernicke, 16
Cal.App.4th 1069, 1076 n.7, 20 Cal.Rptr.2d 481 (1993).
