# 7551. Parentage; exceptions; applications

*Parent and Child Relationship*

parentage; exceptions; applications
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  Except as provided in subdivisions <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  and (c), ina civil
action or proceeding in which parentage is a relevant fact, the
court may, upon its own initiative or upon suggestion made by
or on behalf of any person who is involved, and shall upon
motion of any party to the action or proceeding made at a
time so as not to delay the proceedings unduly, order the
woman who gave birth, the child, and the alleged genetic
parent to submit to genetic testing.
(b)<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Genetic testing shall not be used for any of the
following purposes:
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  To challenge the parentage of a person who is a
parent pursuant to subdivision <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  of Section 7613, except to
resolve a dispute whether the child was conceived tirovigh
assisted reproduction.
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  To challenge the parentage of a person who is a parent
pursuant to Section 7962, except to resolve a dispute whether
the gestational carrier surrogate is a genetic parent.
<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  To establish the parentage of a person who is a donor
pursuant to subdivision <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  or <span class="subsec">(c)</span>  of Section 7613, except to
resolve a dispute whether the child was conceived through
assisted reproduction.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  If the child has a presumed parent pursuant to Section
7540, a motion for genetic testing is governed by Section
7541.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  If the child has a parent whose parentage has been
previously established in a judgment, a request for genetic
testing shall be governed by Section 7647.7.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  A court shall not order genetic testing if the genetic
testing would be used to establish the parentage of a person
who is prohibited under this division from establishing
parentage based on evidence of genetic testing.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Acourt shall not order in utero genetic testing.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  In any case under this division in which genetic testing
is ordered, the following shall apply:
DETERMINATION
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Ifa party refuses to submit to genetic testing, the court
may resolve the question of parentage against that party or
enforce its order if the rights of others and the interests of
justice so require.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The refusal of a party to submit to genetic testing is
admissible in evidence in any proceeding to determine
parentage.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  If two or more persons are subject to court-ordered
genetic testing, the court may order that the testing be
completed concurrently or sequentially.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Genetic testing of a woman who gave birth to a child is
not a condition precedent to the testing of the child and a
person whose genetic parentage of the child is being determined. If the woman is unavailable for genetic testing, the
court may order genetic testing of the child and each person
whose genetic parentage of the child is at issue.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  An order under this division for genetic testing is
enforceable by contempt.
