4250.
Legislative findings and declarations
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  The Legislature finds and declares the following:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Child and spousal support are serious legal obligations.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The current system for obtaining, modifying, and
enforcing child and spousal support orders is inadequate to
meet the future needs of California’s
children
due to
burgeoning caseloads within local child support agencies and
the growing number of parents who are representing them-
selves in family law actions.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The success of California’s child support enforcement
program depends upon its ability to establish and enforce
child support orders quickly and efficiently.

<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  There is a compelling state interest in creating an
expedited process in the courts that is cost-effective and
accessible to families, for establishing and enforcing child
support orders in cases being enforced by the local child
support agency.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  There is a compelling state interest in having a simple,
speedy, conflict-reducing system, that is both cost-effective
and accessible to families, for resolving all issues concerning
children, including support, health insurance, custody, and
visitation in family law cases that do not involve enforcement
by the local child support agency.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  Therefore, it is the intent of the Legislature to: <span class="subsec">(1)</span> 
provide for commissioners to hear child support cases being
enforced by the local child support agency; <span class="subsec">(2)</span>  adopt uniform
and simplified procedures for all child support cases; and <span class="subsec">(3)</span> 
create an Office of the Family Law Facilitator in the courts to
provide education, information, and assistance to parents
with child support issues.
