# Nullity, Dissolution, and Legal Separation

## Section 2251

<div class="source-note">Back to <a href="/division-6-dissolution">Division 6: Nullity, Dissolution, and Legal Separation</a></div>

Status of putative spouse; division of community or quasi-community property 
(a) If a determination is made that a marriage is void or voidable and the court finds that either party or both parties believed in good faith that the marriage was valid, the court shall: 
(1) Declare the party or parties, who believed in good faith that the marriage was valid, to have the status of a putative spouse. 
(2) If the division of property is in issue, divide, in accordance with <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&division=7.">Division 7</a> (commencing with <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=2500.">Section 2500</a>), that property acquired during the union that would have been 

§ 2251 community property or quasi-community property if the union had not been void or voidable, only upon request of a party who is declared a putative spouse under paragraph (1). 
This property is known as “quasi-marital property.” 
(b) If the court expressly reserves jurisdiction, it may make the property division at a time after the judgment. 
(Stats. 
1992, c. 162 (A.B.2650), § 10, operative Jan. 1, 1994. Amended by Stats.2015, c. 196 (A.B.380), § 1, eff. Jan. 1, 2016.)
