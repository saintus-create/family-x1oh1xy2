# 134

Eff. Jan. 1, 2019.)

5, eff. Jan. 1, 2018; Stats.2018, c. 833 (A.B.3176), 32,
eff. Jan. 1, 2019.)
Commentary
In re Cody R., 30 Cal.App.5th 381, 241 Cal.Rptr.3d 399 (2018), held
that habeas corpus in dependency cases is limited to wrongful
withholding of custody of a child, including lack of jurisdiction and
ineffective assistance of counsel. A petition for a writ of habeas
corpus may not be used to challenge a dependent child’s placement.
In re J.W., 26 Cal.App.5th 263, 236 Cal.Rptr.3d 785 (2018), affirmed
a trial court order terminating a mother’s parental rights and
selecting adoption as a permanent plan for a young child who, in his
parents’ care, had suffered attention deficit disorder, anxiety, and
reactive attachment disorder, conditions to which his mother had
been a contributing factor, when the evidence showed he was healthy
and thriving after ten months with his foster parents, who loved him
and wished to adopt him.
Applying subsection (n)<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  and (n)<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  of this section, In re M.M,
235 Cal.App.4th 54, 184 Cal.Rptr.3d 727 (2015), held that failure to
provide notice to a de facto parent of a hearing that might result in
removal of a child from her home deprived her of the opportunity to
request designation as a prospective adoptive parent and to be heard
on the issue of removal. A de facto parent’s possible knowledge that
social services was considering other prospective adoptive parents
does not satisfy the written notice required under this section.
In re Hunter W., 200 Cal.App.4th 1454, 135 Cal.Rptr.3d 355 (2011),
held that a juvenile court abused its discretion when it denied the
request of the parents’ counsel for a brief two-hour delay to locate a
dependent child’s parents, who had already checked in at the court’s
morning calendar call, and thus deprived them of the opportunity to
testify before the court denied their petition under section 388 and
terminated their parental rights under this section.
