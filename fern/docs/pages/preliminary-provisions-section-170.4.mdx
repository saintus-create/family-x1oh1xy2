Concerning Decisionmakers.
Powers of disqualified judges
(a)  A disqualified judge, notwithstanding his or her disqualification may do any of the following:
(1)  Take any action or issue any order necessary to
maintain the jurisdiction of the court pending the assignment
of a judge not disqualified.
(2)  Request any other judge agreed upon by the parties to
sit and act in his or her place.
(3)  Hear and determine purely default matters.
(4)  Issue an order for possession prior to judgment in
eminent domain proceedings.
(5)  Set proceedings for trial or hearing.
(6)  Conduct settlement conferences.
(b)  Notwithstanding paragraph (5)  of subdivision (c)  of
Section 170.3, if a statement of disqualification is untimely
filed or if on its face it discloses no legal grounds for
disqualification, the trial judge against whom it was filed may
order it stricken.

(3)  A party may file no more than one statement of
disqualification against a judge unless facts suggesting new
grounds for disqualification are first learned of or arise after
the first statement of disqualification was filed. Repetitive
statements of disqualification not alleging facts suggesting
new grounds for disqualification shall be stricken by the judge
against whom they are filed.
(d)  Except as provided in this section, a disqualified judge
shall have no power to act in any proceeding after his or her
disqualification or after the filing of a statement of disqualification until the question of his or her disqualification has
been determined.
