### PART 1. DEFINITIONS AND GENERAL PROVISIONS

`3000–3012`

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="§ 3000. Definitions govern">
    Unless the provision or context otherwise requires, the definitions in this chapter govern the construction of this division.

    *(Enacted by Stats. 1992, Ch. 162, Sec. 10. Operative January 1, 1994.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 3011. Best interests of the child">
    (a) In making a determination of the best interests of the child in a proceeding described in Section 3021, the court shall, among any other factors it finds relevant and consistent with Section 3020, consider all of the following: (1) The health, safety, and welfare of the child. (2) A history of abuse by one parent or any other person seeking custody... (3) The nature and amount of contact with both parents... (4) The habitual or continual illegal use of controlled substances or abuse of alcohol... (b) Notwithstanding subdivision (a), the court shall not consider the sex, gender identity, gender expression, or sexual orientation of a parent, legal guardian, or relative in determining the best interests of the child.

    *(Amended by Stats. 2023, Ch. 493, Sec. 2. (SB 599) Effective January 1, 2024.)*
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Anchor id="part-2">

### PART 2. RIGHT TO CUSTODY OF MINOR CHILD

`3020–3204`

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="§ 3020. Public policy">
    (a) The Legislature finds and declares that it is the public policy of this state to ensure that the health, safety, and welfare of children shall be the court's primary concern in determining the best interests of children when making any orders regarding the physical or legal custody or visitation of children... (b) The Legislature finds and declares that it is the public policy of this state to ensure that children have frequent and continuing contact with both parents after the parents have separated... except when the contact would not be in the best interests of the child... (c) When the policies set forth in subdivisions (a) and (b)... are in conflict, a court's order... shall be made in a manner that ensures the health, safety, and welfare of the child and the safety of all family members.

    *(Amended by Stats. 2019, Ch. 551, Sec. 2. (SB 495) Effective January 1, 2020.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 3040. Order of preference in granting custody">
    (a) Custody should be granted in the following order of preference according to the best interest of the child as provided in Sections 3011 and 3020: (1) To both parents jointly... or to either parent... (2) If to neither parent, to the person or persons in whose home the child has been living in a wholesome and stable environment. (3) To any other person or persons deemed by the court to be suitable... (e) This section establishes neither a preference nor a presumption for or against joint legal custody, joint physical custody, or sole custody, but allows the court and the family the widest discretion to choose a parenting plan that is in the best interest of the child...

    *(Amended by Stats. 2022, Ch. 385, Sec. 3. (SB 1182) Effective January 1, 2023.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chapters 4–13 (§§ 3080–3204)">
    - Chapter 4 — Joint Custody (§§ 3080–3089)
    - Chapter 5 — Visitation Rights (§§ 3100–3105)
    - Chapter 6 — Custody Investigation and Report (§§ 3110–3118)
    - Chapter 7 — Action for Exclusive Custody (§§ 3120–3121)
    - Chapter 10 — Appointment of Counsel to Represent Child (§§ 3150–3153)
    - Chapter 11 — Mediation of Custody and Visitation (§§ 3160–3188)
    - Chapter 13 — Supervised Visitation (§§ 3200–3204)
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning title="Health, safety, welfare first">
  Under § 3020(a), a child's <strong>health, safety, and welfare</strong> is the court's
  <em>primary</em> concern. Under § 3040(e), the Code creates no presumption for or against
  any custody form — the best interest of the child controls.
</Warning>
