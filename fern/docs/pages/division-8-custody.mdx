## Custody of Children

  <h3>Jurisdiction</h3>

    <div className="statute-card">
      <div className="section-body">
        <div className="section-meta"><Badge intent="info" minimal>Sec. 3000</Badge></div>
        <p>California courts have jurisdiction to determine custody of a child when the child is a resident of or domiciled in California, or when the child has a significant connection to the state.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  
  <h3>Best interest of the child</h3>

    <div className="statute-card">
      <div className="section-body">
        <div className="section-meta"><Badge intent="info" minimal>Sec. 3011</Badge></div>
        <p>In making a determination regarding the best interest of the child, the court shall consider the health, safety, and welfare of the child, any history of abuse, and the nature and amount of contact with both parents.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  
  <h3>Frequent and continuing contact</h3>

    <div className="statute-card">
      <div className="section-body">
        <div className="section-meta"><Badge intent="info" minimal>Sec. 3020</Badge></div>
        <p><strong>(a)</strong> It is the policy of this state to assure minor children of frequent and continuing contact with both parents after separation or dissolution. <strong>(b)</strong> The court shall not prefer a parent as custodian because of the sex of that parent.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  
  <h3>Presumption favoring joint custody</h3>

    <div className="statute-card">
      <div className="section-body">
        <div className="section-meta"><Badge intent="info" minimal>Sec. 3040</Badge></div>
        <p>In making an order for joint custody, the court shall consider joint custody to be consistent with the best interest of the child. There is a rebuttable presumption that joint custody is in the best interest of the child.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
