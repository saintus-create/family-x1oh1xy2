# 2210. Summary

*Nullity, Dissolution, and Legal Separation*

Summary
10th
Husband
and
Wife

VOIDABLE MARRIAGE
Effect of judgment of nullity; conclusiveness.
Annulment, causes for
A marriage is voidable and may be adjudged a nullity if any
of the following conditions existed at the time of the
marriage:
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  The party who commences the proceeding or on whose
behalf the proceeding is commenced was under 18 years of
age, unless the party entered into the marriage pursuant to
Section 302 or 303.
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The former marriage has been dissolved or adjudged a
nullity before the date of the subsequent marriage.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  The spouse of either party was living and the marriage
with that spouse was then in force and that spouse <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  was
absent and not known to the party commencing the proceeding to be living for a period of five successive years
immediately preceding the subsequent marriage for which the
judgment of nullity is sought or <span class="subsec">(2)</span>  was generally reputed or
believed by the party commencing the proceeding to be dead
at the time the subsequent marriage was contracted.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The former spouse <span class="subsec">(A)</span>  is absent, and not known to the
person to be living for the period of five successive years
immediately preceding the subsequent marriage, or <span class="subsec">(B)</span>  is
generally reputed or believed by the person to be dead at the
time the subsequent marriage was contracted.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  In either of the cases described in paragraph <span class="subsec">(2)</span>  of
subdivision (a), the subsequent marriage is valid until its
nullity is adjudged pursuant to subdivision <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  of Section
2210. (Stats.1992, c. 162 (A.B.2650), § 10, operative Jan. 1,
1994. Amended by Stats.2014, c. 82 (S.B.1306), § 24, eff. Jan.
teal.)
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Either party was of unsound mind, unless the party of
unsound mind, after coming to reason, freely cohabited with
the other as his or her spouse.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  The consent of either party was obtained by fraud,
unless the party whose consent was obtained by fraud
afterwards, with full knowledge of the facts constituting the
fraud, freely cohabited with the other as his or her spouse.
