# General Provisions

## Section 279.6

<div class="source-note">Back to <a href="/marriage">Division 2: General Provisions</a></div>

Protective custody; circumstances; procedures; conflicting custodial orders; court hearing and enforcement 
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  A law enforcement officer may take a child into protective custody under any of the following circumstances: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  It reasonably appears to the officer that a person is likely to conceal the child, flee the jurisdiction with the child, or, by flight or concealment, evade the authority of the court. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  There is no lawful custodian available to take custody of the child. 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  There are conflicting custody orders or conflicting claims to custody and the parties cannot agree which party should take custody of the child. 
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  The child is an abducted child. 
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  When a law enforcement officer takes a child into protective custody pursuant to this section, the officer shall do one of the following: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Release the child to the lawful custodian of the child, unless it reasonably appears that the release would cause the child to be endangered, abducted, or removed from the jurisdiction. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Obtain an emergency protective order pursuant to Part 3 (commencing with <a class="statute-link" href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=6240.">§ 6240</a>) of <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&division=10.">Division 10</a> of the Family Code ordering placement of the child with an interim custodian who agrees in writing to accept interim custody. 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Release the child to the social services agency responsible for arranging shelter or foster care. 
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Return the child as ordered by a court of competent jurisdiction. 
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Upon the arrest of a person for a violation of <a class="statute-link" href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=278.">§ 278</a> or 278.5, a law enforcement officer shall take possession of an abducted child who is found in the company of, or under the control of, the arrested person and deliver the child as directed in subdivision (b). 
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  Notwithstanding any other law, when a person is arrested for an alleged violation of <a class="statute-link" href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=278.">§ 278</a> or 278.5, the court shall, at the time of the arraignment or thereafter, order that the child shall be returned to the lawful custodian by or on a specific date, or that the person show cause on that date why the child has not been returned as ordered. 
If conflicting custodial orders exist within this state, or between this state and a foreign state, the court shall set a hearing within five court days to determine which court has jurisdiction under the laws of this state and determine which state has subject matter jurisdiction to issue a custodial order under the laws of this state, the Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction Act (Part 3 (commencing with <a class="statute-link" href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=3400.">§ 3400</a>) of <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&division=8.">Division 8</a> of the Family Code), or federal law, if applicable. 
At the conclusion of the hearing, or if the child has not been returned as ordered by the court at the time of arraignment, the court shall enter an order as to which custody order is valid and is to be enforced. 
If the child has not been returned at the conclusion of the hearing, the court shall set a date within a reasonable time by which the child shall be returned to the lawful custodian, and order the defendant to comply by this date, or to show cause on that date why he or she has not returned the child as directed. 
The court shall only enforce its order, or any subsequent orders for the return of the child, under subdivision (a) of <a class="statute-link" href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=1219.">§ 1219</a> of the Code of Civil Procedure, to ensure that the child is promptly placed with the lawful custodian. An order adverse to either the prosecution or defense is reviewable by a writ of mandate or prohibition addressed to the appropriate court.
