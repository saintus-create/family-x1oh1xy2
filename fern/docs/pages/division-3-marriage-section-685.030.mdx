# § 685.030

*Money judgments; satisfaction in full; accrual*

Money judgments; satisfaction in full; accrual
of interest; writ of execution
Proc. 1052, 1052.1.
A technical change is also made for conformity with preferred
drafting style. [30 Cal.L.Rev.Comm. Reports 443 (2000)].
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  If a money judgment is satisfied in full pursuant to a
writ
under
this
title,
interest
ceases
to
accrue
on
the
judgment:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  If the proceeds of collection are paid in a lump sum, on
the date of levy.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  If the money judgment is satisfied pursuant to an
earnings withholding order, on the date and in the manner
provided in Section 706.024 or Section 706.028.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  In any other case, on the date the proceeds of sale or
collection are actually received by the levying officer.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  If a money judgment is satisfied in full other than
pursuant to a writ under this title, interest ceases to accrue on
the date the judgment is satisfied in full.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  If a money judgment is partially satisfied pursuant to a
writ under this title or is otherwise partially satisfied, interest
ceases to accrue as to the part satisfied on the date the part is
satisfied.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  For the purposes of subdivisions <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  and (c), the date a
money judgment is satisfied in full or in part is the earliest of
the following times:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The date satisfaction is actually received by the judgment creditor.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The date satisfaction is tendered to the judgment
creditor or deposited in court for the judgment creditor.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The date of any other performance that has the effect
of satisfaction.
<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  The clerk of a court may enter in the Register of
Actions a writ of execution on a money judgment as returned
wholly satisfied when the judgment amount, as specified on
the writ, is fully collected and only an interest deficit of no
more than ten dollars ($10) exists, due to automation of the
continual daily interest accrual calculation. (Added by Stats. .
