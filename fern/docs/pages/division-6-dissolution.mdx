### Part 1. General Provisions

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 2000–2129</Badge>

</Anchor>

This Part governs procedure common to nullity, dissolution, and legal separation: jurisdiction (Chapter 2, §§ 2010–2013), procedural provisions (Chapter 3), attorney's fees (Chapter 3.5), protective and restraining orders (Chapter 4), joinder of pension plans (Chapter 6), restoration of name (Chapter 7), disclosure of assets (Chapter 9), and relief from judgment (Chapter 10).

<Anchor id="part-2">

### Part 2. Void or Voidable Marriage

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 2200–2255</Badge>

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 1 — Void Marriage (§§ 2200–2201)">
    A marriage is void if it is bigamous or incestuous under California law. A judgment of nullity may be entered.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 2 — Voidable Marriage (§§ 2210–2212)">
    A marriage is voidable (not automatically invalid) on grounds such as prior existing marriage, unsound mind, fraud, force, physical incapacity, or minority. The marriage remains valid until a judgment of nullity is entered.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Anchor id="part-3">

### Part 3. Dissolution of Marriage and Legal Separation

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 2300–2452</Badge>

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="§ 2300. Effect of dissolution">
    The effect of a judgment of dissolution of marriage when it becomes final is to restore the parties to the state of unmarried persons.

    *(Enacted by Stats. 1992, Ch. 162, Sec. 10. Operative January 1, 1994.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 2310. Grounds for dissolution or legal separation">
    Dissolution of the marriage or legal separation of the parties may be based on either of the following grounds, which shall be pleaded generally: (a) Irreconcilable differences, which have caused the irremediable breakdown of the marriage. (b) Permanent legal incapacity to make decisions.

    *(Amended by Stats. 2014, Ch. 144, Sec. 9. (AB 1847) Effective January 1, 2015.)*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 3 — Residence Requirements (§§ 2320–2322)">
    A petitioner must meet residence requirements before the court may dissolve the marriage. (See Fam. Code § 2320 et seq.)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="§ 2400. Summary dissolution">
    (a) A marriage may be dissolved by the summary dissolution procedure provided in this chapter if all of the following conditions exist at the time the proceeding is commenced: (1) Either party has met the jurisdictional requirements of Chapter 3 (commencing with Section 2320) with regard to dissolution of marriage. (2) Irreconcilable differences have caused the irremediable breakdown of the marriage and the marriage should be dissolved. (3) There are no children of the relationship of the parties born before or during the marriage or adopted by the parties during the marriage, and neither party, to that party's knowledge, is pregnant. (4) The marriage is not more than five years in duration as of the date of separation of the parties. (5) Neither party has any interest in real property... (6) There are no unpaid obligations in excess of four thousand dollars ($4,000)... (7) The total fair market value of community property assets... is less than twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000)... (8) The parties have executed an agreement setting forth the division of assets and the assumption of liabilities... (9) The parties waive any rights to spousal support. (10) The parties... irrevocably waive their respective rights to appeal... (11) The parties have read and understand the summary dissolution brochure... (12) The parties desire that the court dissolve the marriage. (b) On January 1, 1985, and on January 1 of each odd-numbered year thereafter, the amounts in paragraph (6)... shall be adjusted to reflect any change in the value of the dollar...

    *(Amended by Stats. 2014, Ch. 82, Sec. 28. (SB 1306) Effective January 1, 2015.)*
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  California is a <strong>no-fault</strong> dissolution state. Under § 2310, the only ground that must be pleaded is <em>irreconcilable differences</em>. Fault is not required.
</Note>

### Two paths out of a marriage

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Regular dissolution">
    Filed under Part 3 when there are children, real property, or assets above the summary-dissolution thresholds. Resolves custody, support, and property.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Summary dissolution (§ 2400)">
    A streamlined path for short marriages (≤ 5 years), no children, limited assets (≤ $25,000 community), and limited debt (≤ $4,000). Both parties must agree and waive spousal support.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
