# § 136.2 — In which the parties in a family law case agree to meet with a

<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  “Settlement service(s)” refers to voluntary procedures
in which the parties in a family law case agree to meet with a
neutral third party professional for the purpose of identifying
the issues involved in the case and attempting to reach a
resolution of those issues by mutual agreement.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Duties of settlement service providers
Courts providing settlement services must develop proce-
dures for handling cases involving domestic violence.
In
developing these procedures, courts should consider:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Reviewing court files or, if available, intake forms, to
inform the person providing settlement
services of any
existing protective orders or history ;
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Making reasonable efforts to ensure the safety of
parties when they are participating in services;
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Avoiding negotiating with the parties about using
violence with each other, whether either party should or
should not obtain or dismiss a restraining order, or whether
either party should cooperate with criminal prosecution;
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Providing information and materials that describe the
settlement services and procedures with respect to domestic
violence;
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  Meeting first with the parties separately to determine
whether joint meetings are appropriate in a case in which
RULES OF COURT
there has been a history between the
parties or in which a protective order is in effect;
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  Conferring with the parties separately regarding safety-
related issues and the option of continuing in separate
sessions at separate times if domestic violence is discovered
after services have begun;
<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  Protecting the confidentiality of each party’s times of
arrival, departure, and meeting for separate sessions when
appropriate; and
<span class="subsec">(8)</span>  Providing information to parties about support persons
participating in joint or separate sessions.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  Training and education
All settlement
service providers should participate
in
programs of continuing instruction
in issues related
to
domestic violence, including child abuse.
(Adopted, eff: Jan.
1, 2013.)
