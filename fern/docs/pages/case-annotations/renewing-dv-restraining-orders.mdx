**Citation:** 196 Cal.App.4

th 773 A trial court can only renew a DVRO for five years or permanently. A trial court cannot renew a DVRO for any other amount of time, including, as here, for three years only. ## B. Cases 
 
Hart v. Hart (2025)

**Citation:** 115 Cal.App.5

B. Cases Hart v. Hart (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 571 In this case the trial court renewed a DVRO for only 9 months. The protected party appealed arguing that a five-year period was the minimum allowed by the DVPA. Finding that the trial court does not have discretion under Family Code section 6345 to renew a DVRO for a period of less than 5 years, the Court of Appeal agreed. Noting that the plain language of the DVPA states that a DVRO “may be renewed…either for five or more years, or permanently”, the Court of Appeal reversed the order and instructed the trial court to issue a five-year renewal. Statues used or affected: Family Code sections 6300, 6345

**Statutes:** Family Code sections 6203, 6320, 6345

## Cueto v. Dozier (2015)

**Citation:** 241 Cal.App.4

th 550 The appellate court held the trial court was wrong in finding the petitioner did not have a “reasonable apprehension of future abuse,” and therefore erred when it denied her request to renew the DVRO. This error was in part because the underlying DVRO was precipitated by a “violent incident,” there was “evidence of a long and troubling history of physical abuse,” and circumstances had not changed such that the likelihood of future abuse had diminished. Statutes used or affected: Family Code sections 6203, 6320, and 6345

## Lister v. Bowen (2013)

**Citation:** 215 Cal.App.4

th 319 The appellate court held that any violation of a restraining order, including a non-violent violation, is very serious and gives significant support for renewal of a restraining order. This case also discussed the “burdens” of a renewed DVRO on the restrained party. The case further noted the trial court can and should consider an abusive party’s litigation conduct to decide whether to renew a DVRO. Finally, the case also held that a DVRO is not unconstitutionally overboard by violating the First Amendment right of association. Statutes used or affected: Family Code sections 6203 and 6345

**Statutes:** Family Code sections 6203 and 6345

## Priscila N. v. Leonardo G. (2017)

**Citation:** 17 Cal.App.5

th 1208 The appellate court clarified that all domestic violence restraining orders (DVROs or JVROs) should be treated the same for renewal purposes, and that a DVRO issued by a juvenile court should be considered to have been “issued” under the DVPA for the purpose of renewal.
