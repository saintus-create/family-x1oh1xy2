# 2312 — Permanent legal incapacity to make decisions

Nullity, Dissolution, and Legal Separation

Permanent legal incapacity to make decisions
A marriage may be dissolved on the grounds of permanent
legal incapacity to make decisions only upon proof, including
competent medical or psychiatric testimony, that the spouse
was at the time the petition was filed, and remains, permanently lacking the legal capacity to make decisions. (Stats.
1992, c. 162 (A.B.2650), § 10, operative Jan. 1, 1994. Amended by Stats.2014, c. 144 (A.B.1847), § 10, eff: Jan. I, 2015.)
§ 9, eff. Jan. 1, 2015.)
