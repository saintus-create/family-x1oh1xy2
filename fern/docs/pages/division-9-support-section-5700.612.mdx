Recognition of order modified in another state
If a child-support order issued by a tribunal of this state is
modified by a tribunal of another state which assumed
jurisdiction pursuant to the Uniform Interstate Family Sup-
controls and must be so recognized under Section 5700.207
establishes the aspects of the support order which are
nonmodifiable.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  In a proceeding to modify a child-support order, the
law of the state that is determined to have issued the initial
controlling order governs the duration of the obligation of
support. The obligor’s fulfillment of the duty of support
established by that order precludes imposition of a further
obligation of support by a tribunal of this state.
port Act, a tribunal of this state:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  may enforce its order that was modified only as to
arrears and interest accruing before the modification;
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  may provide appropriate relief for violations of its
order which occurred before the effective date of the
modification; and
<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  On the issuance of an order by a tribunal of this state
modifying a child-support order issued in another state, the
tribunal of this state becomes the tribunal having continuing,
exclusive jurisdiction.
f) Notwithstanding subsections <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  through <span class="subsec">(e)</span>  and Section 5700.201(b), a tribunal of this state retains jurisdiction to
modify an order issued by a tribunal of this state if:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  one party resides in another state; and
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  the other party resides outside the United States.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  shall recognize the modifying order of the other state,
upon registration, for the purpose of enforcement. (Added
by Stats.2015, c. 493 (S.B.646), § 5, eff. Jan. 1, 2016.)
