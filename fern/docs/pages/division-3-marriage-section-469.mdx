UNITED STATES CODE
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  In general.—No credit shall be allowed un-
der this section for any taxable year in the disallow-
ance period.
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Disallowance period.—For purposes of para-
graph (1), the disallowance period is—
<span class="subsec">(i)</span>  the period of 10 taxable years after the
most recent taxable year for which there was a
final determination that the taxpayer’s claim of
credit under this section was due to fraud, and
<span class="subsec">(ii)</span>  the period of 2 taxable years after the
most recent taxable year for which there was a
final determination that the taxpayer’s claim of
credit under this section was due to reckless or
intentional disregard of rules and regulations
(but not due to fraud).
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Taxpayers making improper prior claims.—In
the case of a taxpayer who is denied credit under this
section for any taxable year as a result of the deficiency
procedures under subchapter B of chapter 63, no credit
shall be allowed under this section for any subsequent
taxable year unless the taxpayer provides such informa-
tion as the Secretary may require to demonstrate eligi-
bility for such credit.
<span class="subsec">(L)</span>  Coordination with certain means-tested programs.—
For purposes of—
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  the United States Housing Act of 1937,
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  title V of the Housing Act of 1949,
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  section 101 of the Housing and Urban Develop-
ment Act of 1965,
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  sections 221(d)(3), 235, and 236 of the National
Housing Act, and
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008,
any refund made to an individual (or the spouse of an
individual) by reason of this section shall not be treated as
income (and shall not be taken into account in determining
resources for the month of its receipt and the following
month).
