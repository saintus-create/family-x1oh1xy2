# § 3439.04

*Transfers voidable*

Transfers voidable
as to present and future
creditors; factors to determining intent
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  A transfer made or obligation incurred by a debtor is
voidable as to a creditor, whether the creditor’s claim arose
before or after the transfer was made or the obligation was
incurred, if the debtor made the transfer or incurred the
obligation as follows:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  With actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud any
creditor of the debtor.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Without receiving a reasonably equivalent value in
exchange for the transfer or obligation, and the debtor either:
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  Was engaged or was about to engage in a business or a
transaction for which the remaining assets of the debtor were
unreasonably small in relation to the business or transaction.
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  Intended to incur, or believed or reasonably should
have believed that the debtor would incur, debts beyond the
debtor’s ability to pay as they became due.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  In determining actual intent under paragraph <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  of
subdivision (a), consideration may be given, among other
factors, to any or all of the following:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Whether the transfer or obligation was to an insider.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Whether the debtor retained possession or control of
the property transferred after the transfer.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Whether the transfer or obligation was disclosed or
concealed.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Whether before the transfer was made or obligation
was incurred, the debtor had been sued or threatened with
suit.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  Whether the transfer was of substantially
all the
debtor’s assets.
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  Whether the debtor absconded.
<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  Whether the debtor removed or concealed assets.
<span class="subsec">(8)</span>  Whether the value of the consideration received by the
debtor was reasonably equivalent to the value of the asset
transferred or the amount of the obligation incurred.
<span class="subsec">(9)</span>  Whether the debtor was insolvent or became insolvent
shortly after the transfer was made or the. obligation was
incurred.
<span class="subsec">(10)</span>  Whether the transfer occurred shortly before
or
shortly after a substantial debt was incurred.
<span class="subsec">(11)</span>  Whether the debtor transferred the essential assets of
the business to a lienor that transferred the assets to an
insider of the debtor.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  A creditor making a claim for relief under subdivision
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  has the burden of proving the elements of the claim for
relief by a preponderance of the evidence.
