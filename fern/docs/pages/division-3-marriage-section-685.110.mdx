# 685.110

Commentary

[16 Cal.L.Rev.Comm. Reports 2193 (1982)].
Commentary
Applying this section, In re Marriage of Dalgleish and Selvaggio, 17
Cal.App.5th 1172, 225 Cal.Rptr.3d 900 (2017), review denied, held that
when a stipulated judgment required husband to make an equaliza-
tion payment to wife ten days after a joint appraisal that had not yet
occurred, interest on the payment began to accrue on the date it was
due under the stipulated judgement.
The 2002 amendment of Family Code 155, which exempts
support orders from the operation of subsection <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  of this section,

OF CIVIL ACTIONS
was intended to abrogate the installment-payment holding of Dupont
v. Dupont, 88 Cal.App.4th 192, 105 Cal.Rptr.2d 607 (2001). Dupont
sustained the conclusion of the trial court that a 1978 child support
order requiring a former spouse to pay child support and spousal
support arrearages of $9,000 at the rate of $15 monthly was an
installment judgment within the meaning of subsection (b), Thus,
interest began to accrue with respect to each installment as each
installment became due. In other words, interest did not accrue on
the entire $9,000 judgment from the date that the 1978 order was
entered. Under the 2002 amendment of Family Code 155, interest
accrues on an order requiring the payment of support arrearages
from the time the order was entered.
In re Marriage of McClellan, 130 Cal.App.4th 247, 30 Cal.Rptr.3d 5
(2005), holds that the 2000 amendment to Family Code 155 applies
retroactively because it clarified, rather than altered, existing law.
Accordingly, it applies in calculating the interest due on child support
arrearages that were the subject of arrearage orders entered before
the amendment took effect.
In re Marriage of Hubner, 124 Cal.App.4th 1082, 22 Cal.Rptr.3d 549
(2004), holds that statutory interest on unpaid child support accrues
from the period each payment originally came due during the period
in which the trial court erroneously suspended the parent’s child
support obligation.
