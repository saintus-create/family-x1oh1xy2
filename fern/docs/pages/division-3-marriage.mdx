<Anchor id="part-1">

### Part 1. Validity of Marriage

<SectionRange from="300" to="310" />

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="<Section num="300" /> Marriage is a personal relation / civil contract">
    (a) Marriage is a personal relation arising out of a civil contract between two persons, to which the consent of the parties capable of making that contract is necessary. Consent alone does not constitute marriage. Consent must be followed by the issuance of a license and solemnization as authorized by this division, except as provided by Section 425 and Part 4 (commencing with Section 500). (b) For purposes of this part, the document issued by the county clerk is a marriage license until it is registered with the county recorder, at which time the license becomes a marriage certificate.

    <LegislativeHistory type="Amended" statute="Stats. 2014, Ch. 82, Sec. 2" effective="January 1, 2015" bill="SB 1306" />
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="<Section num="301" /> Capacity to consent">
    Two unmarried persons 18 years of age or older, who are not otherwise disqualified, are capable of consenting to and consummating marriage.

    <LegislativeHistory type="Amended" statute="Stats. 2014, Ch. 82, Sec. 3" effective="January 1, 2015" bill="SB 1306" />
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="<Section num="308" /> Out-of-state marriages">
    A marriage contracted outside this state that would be valid by laws of the jurisdiction in which the marriage was contracted is valid in California.

    <LegislativeHistory type="Amended" statute="Stats. 2016, Ch. 474, Sec. 4" effective="January 1, 2017" bill="AB 2882" />
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Note the language of § 300: marriage is a <strong>civil contract between two persons</strong>. California has recognized same-sex marriage since 2015 <BillRef type="SB" num="1306" year="2015" />, reflected in the amended text above.
</Tip>

<Anchor id="part-2">

### Part 2. Marriage License

<SectionRange from="350" to="360" />

</Anchor>

A marriage license is obtained from the county clerk before solemnization (see § 300(b)). The license becomes a marriage certificate once registered with the county recorder.

---

<Anchor id="part-3">

### Part 3. Solemnization of Marriage

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 400–426</Badge>

</Anchor>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 1 — Persons Authorized to Solemnize (§§ 400–402)">
    A marriage may be solemnized by a priest, minister, rabbi, or authorized person of any religious denomination; by a judge or retired judge; by a commissioner or retired commissioner of civil marriages; or by a county clerk who has been deputized. (See Fam. Code §§ 400–402.)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chapter 2 — Solemnization of Marriage (§§ 420–426)">
    Solemnization requires the presence of the parties, the person officiating, and one or more witnesses (two witnesses if the parties are unmarried minors). A confidential marriage has different witness rules under Part 4.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

---

<Anchor id="part-4">

### Part 4. Confidential Marriage

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 500–536</Badge>

</Anchor>

A confidential marriage (§§ 500–511) is filed confidentially with the county clerk and is not a public record. Notaries may be approved to authorize confidential marriages (§§ 530–536).

---

<Anchor id="part-5">

### Part 5. Remote Marriage

<Badge intent="note" minimal>§§ 550–560</Badge>

</Anchor>

Added to accommodate remote issuance and solemnization of marriages (effective 2023). Reflects the Code's ongoing modernization.
