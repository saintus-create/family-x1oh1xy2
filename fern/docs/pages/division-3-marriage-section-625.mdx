<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The child must be released unless the court finds that continuance in the home of the parent or legal guardian is contrary to the child’s welfare, and one or more of the following grounds for detention exist: 
<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  The child has violated an order of the court; 
<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  The child has escaped from a commitment of the court; 
<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  The child is likely to flee the jurisdiction of the court; 
<span class="subsec">(D)</span>  It is a matter of immediate and urgent necessity for the protection of the child; or 
<span class="subsec">(E)</span>  It is reasonably necessary for the protection of the person or property of another. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  If the child is a dependent of the court under section 300, the court’s decision to detain must not be based on the child’s status as a dependent of the court or the child welfare services department’s inability to provide a placement for the child. 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The court may order the child placed on home supervision under the conditions stated in sections 628.1 and 636, or detained in juvenile hall or in a suitable place designated by the court. 
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  If the court orders the release of a child who is a dependent of the court under section 300, the court must order the child welfare services department either to ensure that the child’s current caregiver takes physical custody of the child or to take physical custody of the child and place the child in a licensed or approved placement. 
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  Required determinations before detention Before detaining the child, the court must determine whether continuance in the home of the parent or legal guardian is contrary to the child’s welfare and whether there are available services that would prevent the need for further detention. 
The court must make these determinations on a case-by-case basis and must state the evidence relied on in reaching its decision. 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  If the court determines that the child can be returned to the home of the parent or legal guardian through the provision of services, the court must release the child to the OF parent or guardian and order that the probation department provide the required services. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  If the child cannot be returned to the home of the parent or legal guardian, the court must state the facts on which the detention is based. 
<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  Required findings to support detention (§ 636) 
If the court orders the child detained, the court must make the following findings on the record and in the written order. 
The court must reference the probation officer’s report or other evidence relied on to make its determinations: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Continuance in the home of the parent or guardian is contrary to the child’s welfare; 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Temporary placement and care is the responsibility of the probation officer pending disposition or further order of the court; and 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Reasonable efforts have been made to prevent or eliminate the need for removal of the child, or reasonable efforts were not made. 
<span class="subsec">(f)</span>  Required orders to support detention (§ 636) 
If the court orders the child detained, the court must make the following additional orders: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  As soon as possible, the probation officer must provide services that will enable the child’s parent or legal guardian to obtain such assistance as may be needed to effectively provide the care and control necessary for the child to return home; and 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The child’s placement and care must be the responsibility of the probation department pending disposition or further order of the court. 
<span class="subsec">(g)</span>  Factors—violation of court order Regarding ground for detention (c)(1), the court must consider: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The specificity of the court order alleged to have been violated; 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The nature and circumstances of the alleged violation; 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The severity and gravity of the alleged violation; 
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Whether the alleged violation endangered the child or others; 
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The prior history of the child as it relates to any failure to obey orders or directives of the court or probation officer; 
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  Whether there are means to ensure the child’s presence at any scheduled court hearing without detaining the child; 
<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  The underlying conduct or offense that brought the child before the juvenile court; and 
<span class="subsec">(8)</span>  The likelihood that if the petition is sustained, the child will be ordered removed from the custody of the parent or guardian at disposition. 
<span class="subsec">(h)</span>  Factors—escape from commitment Regarding ground for detention (c)(2), the court must consider whether or not the child: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Was committed to the California Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation, Division of Juvenile Justice; or a county juvenile home, ranch, camp, forestry camp, or juvenile hall; and 1960 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Escaped from the facility or the lawful custody of any officer or person in which the child was placed during commitment. 
<span class="subsec">(i)</span>  Factors—likely to flee Regarding ground for detention (c)(3), the court must consider whether or not: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The child has previously fled the jurisdiction of. the court or failed to appear in court as ordered; 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  There are means to ensure the child’s presence at any scheduled court hearing without detaining the child; 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The child promises to appear at any scheduled court hearing; 
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  The child has a prior history of failure to obey orders or directions of the court or the probation officer; 
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The child is a resident of the county; 
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  The nature and circumstances of the alleged conduct or offense make it appear likely that the child would flee to avoid the jurisdiction of the court; 
<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  The child’s home situation is so unstable as to make it appear likely that the child would flee to avoid the jurisdiction of the court; and 
<span class="subsec">(8)</span>  Absent a danger to the child, the child would be released on modest bail or own recognizance were the child appearing as an adult in adult court. 
<span class="subsec">(j)</span>  Factors—protection of child Regarding ground for detention (c)(4), the court must consider whether or not: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  There are means to ensure the care and protection of the child until the next scheduled court appearance; 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The child is addicted to or is in imminent danger from the use of a controlled substance or alcohol; and 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  There exist other compelling circumstances that make detention reasonably necessary.

<span class="subsec">(k)</span>  Factors—protection of person or property of another Regarding ground for detention (c)(5), the court must consider whether or not: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The alleged offense involved physical harm to the person or property of another; 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The prior history of the child reveals that the child has caused physical harm to the person or property of another or has posed a substantial threat to the person or property of another; and 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  There exist other compelling circumstances that make detention reasonably necessary. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Restraining orders As a condition of release or home supervision, the court may issue restraining orders as stated in rule 5.630 or orders restraining the child from any or all of the following: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Molesting, attacking, striking, sexually assaulting, or battering, or from any contact whatsoever with an alleged victim or victim’s family; 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Presence near or in a particular area or building; or 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  Associating with or contacting in writing, by phone, or in person any adult or child alleged to have been a Rule 5.762 companion in the alleged offense. 
(Formerly Rule 1475, adopted, eff: Jan. 1, 1998. 
As amended, eff: Jan. 1, 2001; July 1, 2002; Jan. 1, 2006.
