# § 3044 — Presumption against custody for perpetrators of DV

<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  Upon a finding by the court that a party seeking custody of a child has perpetrated domestic violence within the previous five years against the other party seeking custody of the child, or against the child or the child’s siblings, or against a person in subparagraph <span class="subsec">(A)</span>  of paragraph <span class="subsec">(2)</span>  of subdivision <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  of Section 3011 with whom the party has a relationship, there is a rebuttable presumption that an award of sole or joint physical or legal custody of a child to a person who has perpetrated domestic violence is detrimental to the best interest of the child, pursuant to Sections 3011 and 3020. This presumption may only be rebutted by a preponderance of the evidence.

<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  To overcome the presumption set forth in subdivision (a), the court shall find that paragraph <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  is satisfied and shall find that the factors in paragraph (2), on balance, support the legislative findings in Section 3020.

<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The perpetrator has demonstrated that giving sole or joint physical or legal custody of a child to the perpetrator is in the best interest of the child pursuant to Sections 3011 and 3020. In determining the best interest of the child, the preference for frequent and continuing contact with both parents, as set forth in subdivision <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  of Section 3020, or with the noncustodial parent, as set forth in paragraph <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  of subdivision <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  of Section 3040, may not be used to rebut the presumption, in whole or in part.

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Additional factors:

<span class="subsec">(A)</span>  The perpetrator has successfully completed a batterer’s treatment program that meets the criteria outlined in subdivision <span class="subsec">(c)</span>  of Section 1203.097 of the Penal Code.

<span class="subsec">(B)</span>  The perpetrator has successfully completed a program of alcohol or drug abuse counseling, if the court determines that counseling is appropriate.

<span class="subsec">(C)</span>  The perpetrator has successfully completed a parenting class, if the court determines the class to be appropriate.

<span class="subsec">(D)</span>  The perpetrator is on probation or parole, and has or has not complied with the terms and conditions of probation or parole.

<span class="subsec">(E)</span>  The perpetrator is restrained by a protective order or restraining order, and has or has not complied with its terms and conditions.

<span class="subsec">(F)</span>  The perpetrator has committed further acts .

<span class="subsec">(G)</span>  The court has determined, pursuant to Section 6322.5, that the perpetrator is a restrained person in possession or control of a firearm or ammunition in violation of Section 6389, Section 527.9 of the Code of Civil Procedure, or Section 18120 of the Penal Code.

<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  For purposes of this section, a person has “perpetrated domestic violence” when the person is found by the court to have intentionally or recklessly caused or attempted to cause bodily injury, or sexual assault, or to have placed a person in reasonable apprehension of imminent serious bodily injury to that person or to another, or to have engaged in behavior involving, but not limited to, threatening, striking, harassing, destroying personal property, or disturbing the peace of another, for which a court may issue an ex parte order pursuant to Section 6320 to protect the other party seeking custody of the child or to protect the child and the child’s siblings.

<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  For purposes of this section, the requirement of a finding by the court shall be satisfied by, among other things, and not limited to, evidence that a party seeking custody has been convicted within the previous five years, after a trial or a plea of guilty or no contest, of a crime against the other party that comes within the definition contained in Section 6211 and of abuse contained in Section 6203, including, but not limited to, a crime described in subdivision <span class="subsec">(e)</span>  of Section 243 of, or Section 261, 273.5, 422, or 646.9 of, or former Section 262 of, the Penal Code.

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The requirement of a finding by the court shall also be satisfied if a court, whether that court hears or has heard the child custody proceedings or not, has made a finding pursuant to subdivision <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  based on conduct occurring within the previous five years.

<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  When a court makes a finding that a party has perpetrated domestic violence, the court may not base its findings solely on conclusions reached by a child custody evaluator or on the recommendation of the Family Court Services staff, but shall consider any relevant, admissible evidence submitted by the parties.

<span class="subsec">(f)</span>  <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  It is the intent of the Legislature that this subdivision be interpreted consistently with the decision in Jaime G. v. H.L. (2018) 25 Cal.App.5th 794, which requires that the court, in determining that the presumption in subdivision <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  has been overcome, make specific findings on each of the factors in subdivision (b).

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  If the court determines that the presumption in subdivision <span class="subsec">(a)</span>  has been overcome, the court shall state its reasons in writing or on the record as to why paragraph <span class="subsec">(1)</span>  of subdivision <span class="subsec">(b)</span>  is satisfied and why the factors in paragraph <span class="subsec">(2)</span>  of subdivision (b), on balance, support the legislative findings in Section 3020.

<span class="subsec">(g)</span>  In an evidentiary hearing or trial in which custody orders are sought and where there has been an allegation , the court shall make a determination as to whether this section applies prior to issuing a custody order, unless the court finds that a continuance is necessary to determine whether this section applies, in which case the court may issue a temporary custody order for a reasonable period of time, provided the order complies with Sections 3011 and 3020.

<span class="subsec">(h)</span>  In a custody or restraining order proceeding in which a party has alleged that the other party has perpetrated domestic violence in accordance with the terms of this section, the court shall inform the parties of the existence of this section and shall give them a copy of this section prior to custody mediation in the case.

<span class="subsec">(i)</span>  This section shall become effective on January 1, 2026.
