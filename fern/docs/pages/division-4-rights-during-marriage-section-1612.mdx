# Rights and Obligations During Marriage

## Section 1612

<div class="source-note">Back to <a href="/division-4-rights-during-marriage">Division 4: Rights and Obligations During Marriage</a></div>

Subject matter of premarital agreements 
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  Parties to a premarital agreement may contract with respect to all of the following: 
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The rights and obligations of each of the parties in any of the property of either or both of them whenever and wherever acquired or located. 
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The right to buy, sell, use, transfer, exchange, abandon, lease, consume, expend, assign, create a security interest in, mortgage, encumber, dispose of, or otherwise manage and control property. 
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The disposition of property upon separation, marital dissolution, death, or the occurrence or nonoccurrence of any other event. 
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  The making of a will, trust, or other arrangement to carry out the provisions of the agreement. 
_ 
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The ownership rights in and disposition of the death benefit from a life insurance policy. 
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  The choice of law governing the construction of the agreement.

<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  Any other matter, including their personal rights and obligations, not in violation of public policy or a statute imposing a criminal penalty. 
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  The right of a child to support may not be adversely affected by a premarital agreement. 
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Any provision in a premarital agreement regarding spousal support, including, but not limited to, a waiver of it, is not enforceable if the party against whom enforcement of the spousal support provision is sought was not represented by independent counsel at the time the agreement containing the provision was signed, or if the provision regarding spousal support is unconscionable at the time of enforcement. 
An otherwise unenforceable provision in a premarital agreement regarding spousal support may not become enforceable solely because the party against whom enforcement is sought was represented by independent counsel.
