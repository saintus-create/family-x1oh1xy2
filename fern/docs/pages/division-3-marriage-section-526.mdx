Cases in which authorized; restrictions on grant
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  An injunction may be granted in the following cases:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  When it appears by the complaint that the plaintiff is
entitled to the relief demanded, and the relief, or any part
thereof, consists in restraining the commission or continuance
of the act complained of, either for a limited period or
perpetually.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  When it appears by the complaint or affidavits that the
commission or continuance of some act during the litigation
would produce waste, or great or irreparable injury, to a party
to the action.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  When it appears, during the litigation, that a party to
the action is doing, or threatens, or is about to do, or is
procuring or suffering to be done, some act in violation of the
rights of another party to the action respecting the subject of
the action, and tending to render the judgment ineffectual.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  When pecuniary compensation would not afford adequate relief.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  Where it would be extremely difficult to ascertain the
amount of compensation which would afford adequate relief.

<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The party who obtained the temporary restraining

whichever is earlier, serve on the opposing party a copy of the

stating the date, time, and place of the hearing, any affidavits
to be used in the application, and a copy of the points and
authorities in support of the application. The court may for
good cause, on motion of the applicant or on its own motion,
shorten the time required by this paragraph for service on the
opposing party.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  When the matter first comes up for hearing, if the

temporary restraining order.

injunction.
If the opposing party obtains a continuance
under this paragraph, the temporary restraining order shall
remain in effect until the date of the continued hearing.

time for hearing measured from the date of reissuance. No
