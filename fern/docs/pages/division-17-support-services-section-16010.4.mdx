# § 16010.4

*Legislative findings and declarations; informa-*

Legislative findings and declarations; informa-
tion to foster parents and other caregivers; caretaker
interest in providing legal permanency
The Legislature finds and declares all of the following:
<span class="subsec">(a)</span>  Foster parents are one of the most important sources
of information about the children in their care.
Courts,
lawyers, and social workers should have the benefit of
caregivers’ perceptions.
Both federal and state law recognize
the importance of foster parents’ participation in juvenile
court proceedings.
Federal law requires that foster parents
and other caregivers receive expanded opportunities for
notice, the right to participate in dependency court review
and permanency hearings, and the right to communicate
concerns to the courts.
State law similarly provides that
caregivers may submit their concerns to courts in writing.
<span class="subsec">(b)</span>  It is in the children’s best interests that their caregivers
are privy to important information about them.
This infor-
mation is necessary to obtain social and health services for
children, enroll children in school and extracurricular activi-
ties, and update social workers and court personnel about
important developments affecting foster children.
<span class="subsec">(c)</span>  Most school districts and extracurricular organizations
require proof of age before enrolling
a child in their
programs.
Moreover, caregivers are required to obtain a
medical appointment for their foster children within the first
month of receiving the children into their homes.
It would
therefore be in both the children’s and the caregivers’ best
interests to be provided with any available medical informa-
tion, medications and instructions for use, and identifying
information about the children upon receiving the children
into their homes.
<span class="subsec">(d)</span>  Caregivers should have certain basic information in
order to provide for the needs of children placed in their
care, including all of the following:
WELFARE AND INSTITUTIONS CODE
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  The name, mailing address, telephone number, facsim-
ile number, and email address of the child’s social worker and
the social worker’s supervisor.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  The name, mailing address, telephone number, facsim-
ile number, and email address of the child’s attorney and
court-appointed special advocate (CASA), if any.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The name, address, and department number of the
juvenile court in which the child’s juvenile court case is
pending.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  The case number assigned to the child’s juvenile court
case.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  A copy of the child’s birth certificate, passport, or
other identifying documentation of age as may be required
for enrollment in school and extracurricular activities.
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  The child’s State Department of Social Services identi-
fication number.
<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  The child’s Medi-Cal identification number or group
health insurance plan number.
<span class="subsec">(8)</span>  Medications or treatments in effect for the child at the
time of placement, and instructions for their use.
<span class="subsec">(9)</span>  A plan outlining the child’s needs and services, includ-
ing information on family and sibling visitation.
<span class="subsec">(10)</span>  A copy of the health and education summary as
required under Section 16010, with the name and current
contact information
of the person
or persons currently
holding the right to make educational decisions for the child.
<span class="subsec">(e)</span>  Caregivers should have knowledge of all of the follow-
ing:
<span class="subsec">(1)</span>  Their right to receive notice of all review and perma-
nency hearings concerning the child during the placement.
<span class="subsec">(2)</span>  Their right to attend those hearings or submit informa-
tion they deem relevant to the court in writing.
<span class="subsec">(3)</span>  The “Caregiver Information Form” (Judicial Council
Form JV-290), which allows the caregiver to provide infor-
mation directly to the court.
<span class="subsec">(4)</span>  Information about and referrals to any existing ser-
vices, including transportation, translation, training, forms,
and other available services.
<span class="subsec">(5)</span>  The caregiver’s obligation to cooperate with any reuni-
fication, concurrent, or permanent planning for the child.
<span class="subsec">(6)</span>  Any known
siblings or half-siblings of the child,
whether the child has, expects, or desires to have contact or
visitation with any or
all siblings, and how and when
caregivers facilitate the contact or visitation.
<span class="subsec">(7)</span>  The importance of the caregiver’s role in education,
educational protections specific to foster youth under state
and federal law, and the rights and obligations of caregivers
to access and maintain educational and health information,
including the requirements under Sections 49069.3, 49076,
and 56055 of the Education Code and Section 16010 of this
code.
<span class="subsec">(f)</span>  Courts should know, at the earliest possible date, the
interest of the caretaker in providing legal permanency for
the child.
