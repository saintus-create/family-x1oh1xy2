(a)  On granting an injunction, the court or judge must
require an undertaking on the part of the applicant to the
effect that the applicant will pay to the party enjoined any
damages, not exceeding an amount to be specified, the party
may sustain by reason of the injunction, if the court finally
decides that the applicant was not entitled to the injunction.
Within five days after the service of the injunction, the person
enjoined may object to the undertaking.
If the court
determines that the applicant’s undertaking is insufficient and
a sufficient undertaking is not filed within the time required
by statute, the order granting the injunction must be dissolved.
(b)  This section does not apply to any of the following
persons:
(1)  Either spouse against the other in a proceeding for
legal separation or dissolution of marriage.
(2)  The applicant for an order described in
(commencing with Section 6200) of the Family Code.
