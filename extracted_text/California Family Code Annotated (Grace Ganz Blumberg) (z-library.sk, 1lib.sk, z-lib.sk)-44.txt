Under subdivision (a), a waiver that is not enforceable pursuant to
Section 143 may be enforceable if it is shown that the waiver at the
time of execution made a fair and reasonable disposition of the rights
of the surviving spouse or the surviving spouse had, or reasonably
should have had, an adequate knowledge of the property and the
financial obligations of the other spouse.
Subdivision (b) provides a “safety valve” from the liberal standards
of enforceability provided by subdivision (a). It permits the court to
refuse to enforce all or a portion of the waiver if the court finds that
enforcement would be “unconscionable” under the circumstances
existing at the time enforcement is sought. Satisfaction of the
standards of enforceability provided by subdivision (a) should insure
in the vast majority of cases that the waiver was fairly made and
properly enforceable.
However, in the exceptional case, circumstances may have changed in a way that neither party may have
contemplated and enforcement of the waiver in its entirety would
now be unconscionable. In short, subdivision (b) provides a measure
of flexibility. It should be emphasized, however, that this subdivision
is not intended to apply in any but the extraordinary case and never
applies where the conditions of Section 143 are met.

the extent to which the fiduciary standards normally applicable to
